# Template: 05 — Screen

## Purpose of this section in the framework

Section 05 is the **light evaluation** at the end of the second diamond's diverge. It looks at every idea from 04 and selects **at most three** for full evaluation (06–13). There is no minimum: if only one idea is worth evaluating, one is selected; if none is, none is.

This section produces:
- A screening table covering **every** idea from 04, each with a qualitative reason
- The selection (zero to three ideas), plus any **flagged** ideas worth evaluating beyond the three
- An answer for each of the founder's leads
- A **handoff** for each selected idea, including its **make-money threshold**, set from research
- The section's claims-to-verify list

Section 05 **judges without scores.** Its reasons are qualitative and rest on evidence already gathered in 01–04, plus light research. It does not estimate market sizes, unit economics, compliance costs or build effort; those are the evaluation's work (06–12), and doing them here would duplicate it. It generates no new ideas.

## Inputs

- **`00-entry.md`:** the leads, which Task 3 answers.
- **`04-ideation.md`:** the ideas table, coverage, leads and any ideas outside the HMW.
- **`03-brief.md`:** the HMW question, target user, key evidence, key figures and open assumptions.
- **`01-discover.md` and `02-define.md`:** the findings and insights the ideas build on, especially Existing solutions.

## Research activated in this section

**Ordinary web search**, for two purposes only:
- **Light checks while screening:** what incumbents actually offer (features, depth and price, including free tiers), or whether a comparable exists that shows people pay for something similar.
- **Threshold research** for each selected idea (Task 4).

Deep research is not warranted by default. If a threshold cannot be set without it, follow the pause in `SKILL.md`, "Research and deep research".

## Analytical work for this section

### Task 1: Screen every idea

Look at every idea in 04's table, including those marked "outside the HMW", against four questions. Answer each in a few words, with conviction labels where a claim is involved:
1. **Answer:** how directly does it answer the HMW question for the people it serves, given the evidence in 03? (An idea outside the HMW is judged on whether its question is worth pursuing instead; usually it is flagged or not selected.)
2. **Demand signal:** is there evidence, from 01–02 or a comparable, that people pay for something like this?
3. **Distinctiveness:** what does it do that existing solutions don't? Compare what the incumbents **actually do**, not what they claim or what their category suggests. An incumbent offering something similar, even for free, is not the same as offering this: a basic or shallow version leaves room. Record the specific difference.
4. **Obvious dealbreaker:** is anything visible **without new research** that would almost certainly fail a later gate, such as an activity prohibited in that market or a technology that does not exist? Record it in a phrase; this is not a gate.

**Founder fit is not a screening question.** Solo-buildability never decides whether an idea is pursued; it is assessed in 10 to shape the build strategy (solo, with a collaborator, with a partner), outside the go/no-go.

### Task 2: Select at most three

An idea is **worth evaluating** if it answers the HMW question (or a question worth pursuing instead) and has no obvious dealbreaker. Weak or missing demand signals do not exclude an idea on their own; they are recorded and tested in the evaluation.

From the ideas worth evaluating, select **at most three**:
- Prefer the ideas with the strongest answer, demand signal and distinctiveness, in that order.
- **Prefer difference.** If two of the strongest ideas are variants of the same shape (same payer, product and form factor), select the stronger one and consider the next-best different idea. Three variants of one shape would waste the evaluation.

**Same idea, different form factors.** When two or more ideas differ mainly in form factor, compare them head to head on fit before selecting:
- what each assumes about how users find, adopt and use it;
- which parts of the problem each serves well or poorly;
- the local lens: a mobile-first market, dominant messaging platforms, payment and infrastructure assumptions.

Select the best fit and flag the next one if it is close. This is where the framework recommends a form factor; 07 then judges the chosen one in depth.

Then sort the rest:
- **Flagged:** worth evaluating, but beyond the three. One line each on why. Flagged ideas appear in 13; the founder decides whether to run them. Nothing runs automatically.
- **Not selected:** one line each on why.

Apply the validation layer (`SKILL.md`, standing habits) to the selection: if a Low-conviction claim is the only thing keeping an idea out of the three, flag the idea rather than leave it unselected, and name the claim.

If **no idea is worth evaluating**, select none. The recommendation is "no idea selected", and the run goes to 13.

### Task 3: Answer the leads

For each lead in 00 (and the founder's own idea, if 00 described one), state which idea answered it, and whether that idea was selected, flagged or not selected, with the reason. A lead is never left unanswered.

### Task 4: Write the handoff for each selected idea

For each selected idea, write a handoff. The evaluation (06–12) starts from it and does not change it:
- **Name:** from 04. It becomes the idea's folder name: `ideas/<idea-name>/`.
- **The idea:** one sentence, from 04.
- **For whom:** the target user from 03, or the other person involved it serves.
- **What is sold, and who pays, for what and when:** the revenue model 09 will evaluate.
- **Form factor:** what 07 will judge.
- **Market:** the market for every evaluation section.
- **Scale ambition:** from 04, in words.
- **Make-money threshold:** see below.
- **Relies on:** the open assumptions from 03 the idea depends on.

**Setting the threshold.** Research comparable businesses at the idea's scale ambition, in the idea's market or a similar one: what revenue they reach, and how fast. Set the threshold at the revenue that comparable businesses of this kind and scale **typically** reach when they work (a typical outcome, not the best-known success), in a format such as "$X annual revenue by year 3". Use the **revenue the business keeps**: its own revenue, excluding money it passes on to sellers, suppliers or workers (a marketplace's take, not its gross transaction value). For most businesses the two are the same; for marketplaces, resellers and agencies they are not. Where a comparable reports only gross figures, convert them using a stated take rate, with its conviction label. For each threshold, state:
- the comparables it rests on, with citation markers;
- its conviction label;
- one sentence on why this level, not a higher or lower one.

The threshold is a pinned per-idea key figure: 06 and 12 measure against it, and 13 scales Defensibility's durability to it. Never re-derive it later.

## Section-specific content for the four required parts

The four required parts are mandatory per `SKILL.md`.

### Analysis

Five labelled subsections, in order:
1. **Screening table:** every idea from 04, with the four answers and its outcome (selected, flagged or not selected) and a one-line reason.
2. **Selection:** the selected ideas and why they beat the others, including the difference check.
3. **Leads:** the answer for each lead.
4. **Handoffs:** one per selected idea.
5. **Claims to verify:** the section's list.

### Recommendation

One of two:
- **"Proceed to evaluation of [idea names]"**, in the order they will be evaluated (the order they are listed in the selection).
- **"No idea selected"**: the run goes to 13, which records why and lists any flagged ideas.

### Rationale

Three to five sentences: why these ideas, why not the strongest of the rest, and the basis of each threshold in a phrase.

### Challenge pass

The judging form from `SKILL.md`, including "Is there a non-obvious option I dismissed too quickly?", plus the section-specific questions below. Each finding uses the fixed short format: what changed and why, or "no change".

## Challenge pass: section-specific questions

- **Safe-choice check:** were conventional ideas preferred over surprising ones without an evidence-based reason?
- **Anchoring check:** were ideas selected because they are close to the founder's leads or framing, rather than on the four questions?
- **Pre-judging check:** did any reason rest on a judgement that belongs to the evaluation (a market size, a cost estimate, the founder's skills)? If so, remove it.
- **Variant check:** are the selected ideas genuinely different shapes?
- **Threshold check:** are the comparables really at the same scale and in a similar market? Is the threshold set by typical outcomes, or pulled up by one famous success? Would a reasonable person set it much higher or lower on the same evidence?

## Word cap

**Hard maximum (provisional): 1,800 words, excluding citation markers and the source list.** The screening table is compact (a few words per cell); the handoffs carry most of the text.

## Downstream dependencies

What later sections consume from section 05:
- **06–12** start from each selected idea's handoff and never change it. **06** scores Problem severity for the handoff's "For whom" and measures market size against its threshold. **07** judges its form factor. **09** evaluates its revenue model. Every section uses its market.
- **12** compares the financial projections against the threshold.
- **13 (Decision)** scales Defensibility's durability to each threshold, and lists the flagged ideas, the answers to the leads and, if no idea was selected, why.
