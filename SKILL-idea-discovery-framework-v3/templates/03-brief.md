# Template: 03 — Brief

## Purpose of this document in the framework

`03-brief.md` is the **midpoint**: the handoff between the two diamonds. Section 02 writes it at the end of the first diamond. Everything after it (ideation, the screen and the evaluation of each idea) starts from this brief.

It states the chosen problem and what is known and unknown about it. It contains **no solution and no make-money threshold**: solutions come from 04, and each idea's threshold comes from 05.

03 is a **handoff document**, like 00. It does not carry the four required parts (Analysis, Recommendation, Rationale, Challenge pass); the reasoning behind it lives in 02. *(See `SKILL.md`, "Required documentation".)*

## How 02 writes it

- **Copy, don't re-derive.** Every statement in 03 comes from 01 or 02 and keeps its conviction label and its source. Citation markers are renumbered to match 03's own source list. If writing 03 exposes a gap, fix it in 02 first.
- **Leave the leads out.** The founder's form-factor and market leads stay in 00. They are not dropped: 04 must turn each lead into at least one idea, and 05 must say whether each was selected and, if not, why. Keeping them out of 03 keeps the brief about the problem, not about any particular answer to it.
- **Don't restate the analysis.** Insights, themes and the comparison of opportunity areas stay in 02, where 04 reads them directly.

## Template

```
# Brief: [WORKING NAME]

## How might we
[The HMW question from 02, word for word.]

## Who it is for
**Primary target user:** [role or identity; relevant characteristics; how often they meet the
problem; what they do today]
**Other people involved:** [other user types or sides named in 02, one line each; ideas in 04 may
serve them instead]

## Why this problem
[Two or three sentences: what the pain or desire is, in the target user's terms, and why 02
chose it. State the overall conviction. If the choice rests on Low-conviction evidence, the
first sentence says so.]

## Key evidence
[The three to six findings that carry the choice, one line each, with conviction label and
citation marker. Include at least one finding on how the problem is handled today.]

## Key figures (pinned)
[The problem-level quantities later sections will reuse, e.g., how many people have the
problem, what they spend on it today. One line each, with conviction and marker. Later
sections reuse these numbers exactly; they never re-derive them. Write "none" if there are
none.]

## Open assumptions
[The open assumptions from 01 that bear on the chosen problem, plus any 02 identified while
choosing. Assumptions about problems that were not chosen are left out.]
| Assumption | Why desk research could not settle it | Cheapest test (who, how, rough time and cost) |
|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... |

## Sources
[Only the sources cited above, numbered to match the markers.]
```

## Word cap

**Hard maximum (provisional): 600 words, excluding citation markers and the source list.** The brief is a summary that 04–13 will read repeatedly; anything longer belongs in 02.

## Downstream dependencies

What later sections consume from 03:
- **04 (Ideation)** generates ideas against the HMW question, for the primary target user or the other people involved.
- **05 (Screen)** judges ideas against the HMW question and the target user.
- **06** uses the target user, key evidence and key figures when scoring Problem severity, adding what is specific to each idea.
- **Every section from 04 on** reuses the pinned key figures exactly.
- **13 (Decision)** carries the open assumptions and their cheapest tests into its summary, and builds any validate-first verdict on them, adding only assumptions specific to that idea.
