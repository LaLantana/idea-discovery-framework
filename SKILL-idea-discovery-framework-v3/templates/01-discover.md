# Template: 01 — Discover

## Purpose of this section in the framework

Section 01 is the **first diamond's diverge**. It opens up the problem space described in `00-entry.md` before anything is narrowed down. Throughout the first diamond, a "problem" includes a **desire**: something people want and cannot easily get, not only a pain. Its job is to find out what is actually going on: who is involved, who has which pain, how they cope today, what constrains a solution, and how many different ways the problem can be framed.

This section produces:
- The assumptions inside 00, made explicit, with the research question that tests each one
- Research findings grouped by topic, conviction-labelled and cited
- A set of **problem framings**: different problem statements that each point at a different business
- **Open assumptions**: what only primary research could settle, each with the cheapest test
- The section's claims-to-verify list

Section 01 **generates; it does not judge.** It assigns no scores, ranks no framings, recommends no solution and cannot kill. Choosing between framings is section 02's job. If a finding seems to make the problem weak, record the finding; 02 decides what it means.

## Inputs

- **`00-entry.md`:** the potential problem (what was observed, where, how the founder knows) and any leads.
- **Leads are assumptions to test, not constraints.** A market lead narrows nothing: research wherever the problem exists, and report where the evidence of it is strongest, including whether that is the lead's market. (Choosing a market is 04's job; 01 only reports.) A form-factor lead plays no part in 01 (form factors are generated in 04); record it and leave it.
- **The founder's observation is first-hand evidence.** Record it as such, and label it by evidence quality like any other finding: it shows that the problem occurred, but on its own it does not show how widespread or severe it is.

## Research activated in this section

**Ordinary web search, run in parallel by agents where useful.** It needs no confirmation. Give each research agent one topic cluster (Task 2) and the instruction to return findings with sources and conviction labels. If agents are not available in the host, research the clusters one at a time; the output is the same.

**Prefer sources that carry users' own voices:** forums, community threads, reviews and complaints, social posts, job postings, support forums, public comments on regulation. These are the closest a desk run gets to primary research. Balance them with authoritative sources (official statistics, registries, regulators, credible industry reports) for anything quantitative.

**Look beyond the founder's geography.** Research the problem wherever it shows up. Apply the local lens (`SKILL.md`, "Avoid US/global defaults") to each geography the evidence points at, not only to the market lead.

**Deep research** (the dedicated research tool, or a structured multi-source investigation) is not the default. If it appears warranted, pause and ask the founder in a plain message (a sanctioned pause; see `SKILL.md`, "Pauses during a run"). If the answer is no, continue with ordinary search and note the limitation.

Conviction-label by evidence quality, cite at the point of use, and add every decision-relevant external fact to the claims-to-verify list (`SKILL.md`, standing habits).

## Analytical work for this section

### Task 1: Rip the entry

Restate the problem from 00 in one or two sentences. Then list the assumptions packed inside it. Typical ones:
- **Who:** that the people named are the ones who have the problem, and that they are the only ones.
- **What:** that the observed symptom is the problem, rather than a symptom of something upstream.
- **Severity:** that it matters enough to the people who have it that they would change behaviour or pay.
- **Unsolved:** that nothing adequate already exists.
- **Where:** that the observed place (and any market lead) is where the problem is, or is worst.
- **Why now:** that something makes this problem newly solvable or newly painful.

For each assumption, write the research question that would test it. Do not accept the framing in 00 as given; it is one observation, not a definition.

### Task 2: Cluster topics

Group the research questions from Task 1 into **topic clusters**, one per research agent. Typical clusters, to adapt to the problem:
- **Actors:** everyone involved on every side (who experiences the pain, who causes it, who pays today, who benefits from the status quo, who regulates) and what each is trying to get done.
- **Current behaviour:** what people do today: workarounds, substitutes, doing nothing, and how painful each is.
- **Existing solutions:** who already addresses any part of the problem, for whom, at what price, and where they fall short.
- **Constraints:** law and regulation, technology, money, incumbents, culture.
- **Geography:** where the problem exists, how it differs between places, and where the evidence is strongest.
- **Change:** what has shifted recently (technology, regulation, behaviour, a competitor's exit) and what it changes.

Each cluster gets a short list of the questions it must answer. A cluster with no questions is dropped; a question that fits no cluster gets its own.

### Task 3: Research each cluster

Run the research per cluster (Research activated, above). Record findings under their cluster as short, sourced statements, each with its conviction label. Findings may contradict each other or the founder's observation. Keep the contradictions visible; do not resolve them here.

### Task 4: Brainstorm problem framings (agents)

Run an agent brainstorm on **problem framings**: problem statements, each pointing at a different business. Give the agents distinct lenses so they don't converge (without agents, run one pass per lens), for example:
- the problem as each actor from the Actors cluster experiences it;
- the problem behind the problem (the cause upstream of the observed symptom);
- the other side of the market (the party who would benefit if the pain were solved);
- a different geography from the evidence;
- the contrarian lens ("this is not actually a problem, because…").

**Rules:**
- **A framing is a problem, not a solution.** "Restaurant owners cannot predict which staff will leave" is a framing; "a churn-prediction app" is not. Rewrite any solution as the problem it would solve.
- **Breadth, not count.** There is no minimum number of framings. Every actor identified in the Actors cluster must have been considered as the one with the problem, and at least one framing must address the problem behind the problem.
- **Each framing cites the findings it rests on.** A framing with no supporting finding is marked as speculative (L).
- **No ranking, no scoring.** 02 chooses.

### Task 5: List the open assumptions

List what the desk research could not settle and only primary research could (for example: whether a specific group would pay, how often a behaviour really happens, whether a workaround is as painful as forums suggest). For each one, state:
- the assumption;
- why desk research could not settle it;
- the **cheapest test** that would: who to ask or observe, how, and a rough time and cost.

These travel with the problem through 03 to the end of the run. They are not resolved in 01.

## Section-specific content for the four required parts

The four required parts (Analysis, Recommendation, Rationale, Challenge pass) are mandatory per `SKILL.md`. As a **diverge section**, 01 adapts them:

### Analysis

Six labelled subsections, in order:
1. **Entry, ripped:** the restated problem and its assumptions, each with its research question.
2. **Topic clusters:** the clusters and their questions.
3. **Findings by cluster:** sourced, conviction-labelled findings.
4. **Problem framings:** each framing with the findings it rests on.
5. **Open assumptions:** each with its cheapest test.
6. **Claims to verify:** the section's list.

### Recommendation

Always **"Proceed to 02."** Section 01 cannot kill.

### Rationale

Two or three sentences: whether the research covers every cluster and every actor, and anything 02 should know about the findings' overall quality (for example, "user-voice evidence is thin outside Colombia"). If deep research was declined, or a cluster could not be researched, say so here.

### Challenge pass

The diverge form, asking **"what did we miss?"** rather than trying to break a conclusion (01 has none). Use the fixed short format for each question: **what changed and why, or "no change."**
- **Missing actors:** is there an actor on any side of the problem that was not researched?
- **Untested assumptions:** did any assumption from 00, including a lead, pass through without being tested?
- **Disguised solutions:** is any framing really a solution?
- **Missing voices:** did the research hear from the people who have the problem, or only about them?
- **Geography:** did the evidence point at a place the research did not follow?
- **Facts:** for each load-bearing fact on the claims-to-verify list, is the evidence real, and is it cited? (The standard fact-verification question from `SKILL.md`.)

## Word cap

**Hard maximum (provisional): 1,500 words for the whole file, excluding citation markers and the source list.** Before the run finishes, the word count of this file is checked; if it is over the cap, it is rewritten to fit. Findings are short statements, not prose paragraphs. Framings are one sentence each plus their supporting references.

## Downstream dependencies

What later sections consume from section 01:
- **02 (Define)** clusters the findings into insights and themes, turns the framings into opportunity areas, and chooses one how-might-we question. It uses no evidence that 01 did not gather, except for targeted gap-filling.
- **03 (Brief)** carries forward the evidence behind the chosen problem and the open assumptions with their cheapest tests.
- **04 (Ideation)** uses the Actors, Existing solutions and Geography findings as raw material for its variation types (who pays, which side of the market, geography).
- **05 (Screen)** may use the Existing solutions findings as comparables when it researches each selected idea's make-money threshold.
- **06** reuses the Current behaviour, Existing solutions and Constraints findings (substitutes, workarounds) instead of researching them again, and draws on the evidence when scoring Problem severity for each idea.
- **08** starts from the Constraints findings (law and regulation) and adds only what is specific to each idea.
