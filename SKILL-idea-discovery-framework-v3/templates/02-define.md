# Template: 02 — Define

## Purpose of this section in the framework

Section 02 is the **first diamond's converge**. It makes sense of what 01 found and chooses the one problem the rest of the run will work on. It moves from findings to insights, from insights to themes, from themes to opportunity areas, and ends with **one how-might-we (HMW) question** and the target user it is for.

This section produces:
- Insights drawn from 01's findings
- Themes that group the insights
- Opportunity areas, each traced to 01's problem framings and to the evidence behind it
- The choice of one opportunity area, with the reasons, plus any **flagged** areas that were nearly as strong
- The HMW question and its target user
- `03-brief.md`, the midpoint brief (see template 03)

Section 02 **judges without scores.** It chooses on conviction-labelled evidence, qualitatively. It does not score the problem (Problem severity is scored per idea in 06), generate ideas or recommend solutions.

Section 02 **always chooses and never stops the run.** Weak evidence is not a reason to stop exploring: when the best area rests on Low-conviction evidence, 02 still chooses it and says so plainly.

**"Problem" includes desire.** An opportunity area can be a pain people want removed, or something they want and cannot easily get. Both are judged the same way.

## Inputs

- **`01-discover.md`:** the findings by cluster, the problem framings, the open assumptions and the claims to verify.
- **`00-entry.md`:** for reference only. The founder's framing is one observation among the findings. It is not a default to return to (see the anchoring check in the challenge pass).

## Research activated in this section

02 works mainly from 01's research. **Targeted gap-filling** with ordinary web search is allowed when a choice between opportunity areas hinges on a specific missing fact. Record any new finding in the Analysis with its source and conviction label, the same way 01 does. Do not reopen 01's clusters wholesale.

Deep research is not warranted by default. If a choice hinges on a question ordinary search cannot settle, follow the pause in `SKILL.md`, "Research and deep research".

## Analytical work for this section

### Task 1: Draw insights

Turn 01's findings into **insights**, numbered I1, I2… so later sections can refer to them. A finding is what was observed ("restaurant owners post about staff leaving without notice"). An insight is what it reveals about people's needs or behaviour ("owners can absorb turnover; what they cannot absorb is not knowing in advance"). Each insight:
- states one thing the evidence shows that is not obvious from any single finding;
- cites the findings it rests on;
- carries its own conviction label: how directly the cited findings support it. An interpretation that goes beyond what the findings show is Low.

Keep contradictions from 01 visible: an insight may be "the evidence disagrees on X", if that disagreement matters.

### Task 2: Group into themes

Group the insights into **themes**: patterns that several insights point at. A theme names the pattern in one line and lists its insights. An insight that fits no theme is kept on its own if it matters, and dropped with a one-line reason if it does not.

### Task 3: Define opportunity areas

Turn the themes and 01's problem framings into **opportunity areas**. Each one states:
- **Who** has the pain or desire (the people themselves, not the people who would sell to them).
- **What** it is, in their terms.
- **The evidence** that it exists and matters to them, with conviction labels. Evidence from the people themselves counts more than evidence about them.
- **Signs anyone pays** to solve it today (money spent on workarounds, existing paid solutions, budgets, job postings), or the explicit absence of such signs.
- **How well it is served now**, drawing on 01's Existing solutions and Current behaviour findings.

**Every framing from 01 is accounted for.** Each one either becomes an opportunity area, merges into one (say which), or is set aside with a one-line reason. Nothing disappears silently.

**Every opportunity area traces to at least one framing from 01.** 02 refines and merges framings; it does not invent them. If a theme points at a problem that no framing covers, add it as an area marked **"surfaced in 02"**, and record it in the challenge pass as something 01 missed.

### Task 4: Choose one opportunity area

Compare the opportunity areas side by side on three questions, qualitatively and without scores:
1. **Evidence of pain or desire:** how strong and how direct is the evidence that it exists and matters? Evidence from the people themselves is more direct than evidence about them.
2. **Signs of payment:** is there evidence that anyone pays, or would pay, to solve it?
3. **Gap:** how poorly is it served today?

Choose **one** area. State why it beats each of the others in a sentence per area.

**In a run of a flagged area** (`SKILL.md`, "Flagged, not run"), the founder has already chosen it from the original run's flagged list. Reuse the original 02's insights, themes and comparison by reference, record the founder's pick and the date of the original 01, carry forward the original run's other flagged areas as this run's flagged list, and go on to Task 5.

Then sort the remaining areas:
- **Flagged:** nearly as strong as the chosen one on the three questions. List each with a one-line reason. Flagged areas appear in 13; the founder decides whether to run them. Nothing runs automatically.
- **Set aside:** the rest, each with a one-line reason.

**When all the evidence is weak.** If even the chosen area rests only on Low-conviction evidence, choose it anyway. State this in the Rationale, and make sure 03 carries the open assumptions and cheapest tests that would strengthen or overturn it.

Apply the validation layer (`SKILL.md`, standing habits) to the choice: identify the low-conviction claims the choice rests on, and validate them or record them as accepted risk.

### Task 5: Write the HMW question and the target user

Write **one HMW question** for the chosen area: "How might we [help / enable] [who] [achieve or avoid what] [in what situation]?" It must:
- **name no solution:** no product, channel, technology or business model;
- **be narrow enough to act on:** not "how might we improve restaurants";
- **be broad enough to allow different ideas:** if only one product could answer it, it is a solution in disguise.

Then define the **target user** precisely:
- **Role or identity:** what makes them this user.
- **Relevant characteristics:** only those that affect the pain (location, size, income, technical literacy).
- **Frequency:** how often they meet the problem.
- **Current behaviour:** what they do today when it happens.

If the area involves more than one distinct user type, name the primary one and list the others. Ideas in 04 may serve a different side; 06 then defines and scores that idea's own user.

### Task 6: Write the midpoint brief

Write `03-brief.md` following template 03: the HMW question, the target user, the evidence behind the chosen area (with the problem-level key figures pinned), and the open assumptions with their cheapest tests. It contains no solution and no threshold.

## Section-specific content for the four required parts

The four required parts are mandatory per `SKILL.md`.

### Analysis

Seven labelled subsections, in order:
1. **Insights:** each with its supporting findings and conviction.
2. **Themes:** each with its insights.
3. **Opportunity areas:** each with who, what, evidence, signs of payment and how well it is served, plus the framing traceability (became / merged / set aside).
4. **Choice:** the three questions applied, the chosen area and why it beats each other area, the flagged areas and the set-aside areas.
5. **HMW and target user.**
6. **Gap-filling research:** any new findings, with sources (or "none").
7. **Claims to verify:** the section's list.

### Recommendation

Always **"Proceed."** 02 has written `03-brief.md` (Task 6), and the run continues to 04.

### Rationale

Three to five sentences: the chosen area, the evidence that decided it, its weakest point, and why the flagged areas lost. If the choice rests on Low-conviction evidence, say so in the first sentence.

### Challenge pass

The judging form from `SKILL.md`, including "Is there a non-obvious option I dismissed too quickly?", plus the section-specific questions below. Each finding uses the fixed short format: what changed and why, or "no change".

## Challenge pass: section-specific questions

- **Anchoring check:** was the chosen area chosen on the evidence, or because it is closest to the founder's framing in 00? Would the same area win if 00 had described a different problem in the same space?
- **Solution in disguise:** does the HMW question imply one particular product, channel or model?
- **Scope check:** is the HMW so broad that anything fits, or so narrow that only one idea fits?
- **Voice check:** is the chosen area evidenced by the people themselves, or only by people who talk about them?
- **Payment check:** are the "signs anyone pays" real spending, or inferred from the size of the pain?
- **Weak-evidence check** (when the choice rests on Low conviction): is the evidence weak because the pain or desire is absent, or because desk research cannot reach it? Say which in the Rationale.

## Word cap

**Hard maximum (provisional): 1,200 words for the whole file, excluding citation markers and the source list.** Insights and themes are one line each with their references; the comparison in Task 4 can be a small table.

## Downstream dependencies

What later sections consume from section 02:
- **03 (Brief)** is written by 02 and carries the HMW question, target user, evidence, key figures and open assumptions forward.
- **04 (Ideation)** generates ideas against the HMW question, using the insights and themes as raw material, and may serve user types other than the primary one.
- **05 (Screen)** judges ideas against the HMW question and the target user.
- **06** uses the target user definition and the chosen area's evidence when scoring Problem severity. For an idea that serves a different user, 06 defines and scores that user instead.
- **13 (Decision)** lists the flagged areas, and repeats the warning if the choice rested on Low-conviction evidence.
