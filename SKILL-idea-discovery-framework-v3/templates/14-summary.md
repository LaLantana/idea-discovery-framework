# Template: 14 — Run summary

## Purpose of this document in the framework

`14-summary.md` is the **record of the run**. It is written **last**, once for the whole problem, after 13. It tells a reader who did not follow the run (the founder months later, or someone they share it with) three things in plain language:
- **what was researched**, and what was not;
- **what was learned**, including what holds for the problem space whatever happened to the ideas;
- **what was decided** at each step, by whom, and on what basis.

13 says what to do. 14 says how the run got there.

**14 summarises only.** It adds no research, scores, judgements or recommendations, and it never re-argues a decision. Every statement traces to a section and keeps that section's conviction label. Pinned figures are reused exactly.

14 is a **record document**, like the handoff documents 00 and 03. It does not carry the four required parts (Analysis, Recommendation, Rationale, Challenge pass); it ends with a short faithfulness check instead. *(See `SKILL.md`, "Required documentation".)*

**14 is always written,** whatever the outcome: no idea selected at 05, every idea killed, or one or more ideas proceeding.

## Inputs

All of 00–13. 14 reads them. It changes an earlier file only through an erratum (see "How to write it") or a length rewrite (Task 4), never to make the summary read better.

## Research activated in this section

None.

## How to write it

- **Copy, don't re-derive.** Take each statement from the section that made it, with its conviction label.
- **Cite sections, not sources.** Each statement carries a section reference, e.g. "§02 I3", "§06 Idea B", "§13". Use the § sign so a reference cannot be mistaken for a numbered source marker. The source lists stay in the sections. Section references count as citation markers and are excluded from the word count.
- **Write for an outsider.** Avoid internal labels on their own: say what a gate or an insight number means in a few words the first time it appears.
- **Point, don't repeat.** Verdict details, validate-first payloads, open assumptions and claims to verify live in 13. 14 names the outcome in one line and points there.
- **If writing 14 exposes a discrepancy** (a figure that drifted, a decision recorded differently in two places), do not fix it in 14. Correct it at its source as an erratum (`SKILL.md`, "Post-decision amendments"), then write 14 from the corrected record.

## Tasks

### Task 1: What was researched

- **The problem space (01):** (in a run of a flagged area, 01 is the original run's, linked; give its date) each topic cluster researched, with one line on what the research found; the number of problem framings considered, naming those 02's opportunity areas were built from; whether deep research was run (a founder's answer to the pause goes in the decision trail, not here).
- **Each evaluated idea (05–12):** one line on what its evaluation researched, such as which comparable businesses set its threshold (05), how the market was sized, which incumbents were examined and which rules were checked. Stop at the section where the idea stopped.
- **The limits:** no primary research was done (it is out of scope); name any line of research a section recorded as unanswered by desk research. The full list of open assumptions is in 13; give its count and point there.

### Task 2: What was learned

- **About the problem (02):** the three to six insights 02 cites for its choice, one line each, keeping their numbers and conviction labels.
- **Key figures:** a small table of the pinned figures from 03, each evaluated idea's threshold (05) and its market estimate (06), plus any other per-idea figure listed in a section's Pinned figures line that the decision turned on.

| Figure | Value | Conviction | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

- **Findings that hold beyond the ideas:** up to five findings from 05–12 that the section itself states about the problem space rather than about one idea (a rule that applies to anyone, the real depth of an incumbent's offer, a channel's typical cost, a capability limit). Do not generalise an idea-specific finding; that would be a new claim. Start from the "what was learned" lines in 13's killed-idea blocks. One line each, with section and conviction. These are what the run leaves behind for anyone revisiting this problem. Write "none" if there are none.

### Task 3: What was decided

A **decision trail** in run order, one row per decision actually made. Gates that did not fire are not decisions and get no row.

| Step | Decision | Basis (one line) | Conviction | Decided by | Section |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | Claude / founder | ... |

Include:
- **02:** the opportunity area chosen, and how many were flagged instead.
- **05:** the ideas selected (out of how many generated in 04), how many were flagged, and how the leads were answered.
- **Each evaluated idea:** one row for its outcome: where it stopped and why (killed or parked), or its verdict from 13 with the decisive driver. Add a row for any gate that fired without ending the idea (a route to a partner or a different legal structure). For an idea that passed 11, name the differentiation hypothesis 07 settled on and the go-to-market strategy 11 recommended.
- **Every founder decision:** the answer to a deep-research pause, a kill escalation or a financial-contradiction escalation, a checkpoint choice, or setting an idea aside.

Close with one sentence pointing to 13 for what to do next.

**If no idea was selected at 05,** the trail ends at 05, with its reason.

### Task 4: Faithfulness check, then count the words

**Faithfulness check.** Record a one-line result for each:
1. **Traceable:** every statement has a section reference.
2. **Nothing new:** no claim, figure, score or judgement appears that is not in its section.
3. **Labels and figures kept:** conviction labels and pinned figures match their sources.
4. **Matches 13:** every outcome in the trail matches 13's verdicts and stop-points.
5. **Even-handed:** proceeding and killed ideas are summarised with the same care; a killed idea keeps what it taught.

Fix any failure in 14 itself, unless the source is wrong (then see "How to write it").

**Word counts.** This is the run's last step. Count every section file written in the run, 01–14 including each idea's 06–12 (00 is the founder's, and a linked 01 from an original run is not counted), excluding citation markers and source lists, against its template's cap. Rewrite any file over its cap without changing its content's substance; if 13 is rewritten, re-run check 4. Record the counts in a short table at the end of this file.

## Word cap

**Hard maximum (provisional): 1,000 words, excluding section references, the faithfulness check and the word-count table.** 14 is a summary; anything longer belongs in the sections it points to.

## Downstream dependencies

14 is the last file written in a run, and nothing in the run reads it. **If the run is amended** after the decision (an erratum, or a flagged idea run later), 14 is updated in the same pass as 13.
