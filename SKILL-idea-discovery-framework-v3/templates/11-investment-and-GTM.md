# Template: 11 — Investment and GTM

## Purpose of this section in the framework

Section 11 works out **how this idea would reach its customers** and **how much capital it takes to launch and reach early traction**. It is where the **GTM gate** can fire. If it fires, 12 is skipped.

This section produces:
- 2–4 candidate go-to-market (GTM) strategies for this idea, evaluated against its unit economics, the traction window, capital intensity, execution capacity and the market
- The recommended GTM strategy (and any viable alternates)
- The **initial investment**: build, compliance, GTM and operating costs for the launch-and-early-traction window
- An evaluation against the GTM gate, including the adjustment attempt
- A recommendation: proceed to 12, or a kill of this idea at the GTM gate

Section 11 scores no commercial dimension; its outputs feed 12 and 13.

**What 11 generates, and what it doesn't.** GTM strategies are generated here because how to reach customers is not one of 04's variation types. Choosing a channel is part of evaluating *this* idea. 11 does **not** change who the idea is for, what is sold, who pays, the payment pattern or the market (`SKILL.md`, "No alternatives after the screen").

## Inputs

- **05's handoff:** the idea, who it serves and the market.
- **09:** the revenue model, pricing and unit economics: what acquisition cost the model can afford.
- **06:** the substitutes, choice dimensions and user definition: where these people already are and how they choose.
- **07:** the form factor (app stores, search, messaging platforms and so on).
- **08 and 09:** compliance costs, one-time and ongoing, including any partner or structure route's fixed fees and setup costs.
- **10:** build time, build capital, partner costs, and the founder's execution capacity.

## Research activated in this section

Web search is **mandatory**. It must produce, at minimum:
- realistic customer acquisition cost (CAC) benchmarks for comparable products **in the idea's market**;
- channel cost benchmarks (CPC, CPM, content costs, sales-motion costs by segment) for the channels under consideration;
- recent shifts in channel effectiveness (ad-platform changes, organic reach, partner ecosystems);
- how 06's incumbents actually acquire customers, where public.

Deep research is warranted when:
- GTM in this market or category is unclear;
- very different strategies need quantitative comparison;
- the recommended GTM depends on a channel or partner ecosystem that needs more than surface search.

Follow the pause in `SKILL.md`, "Research and deep research".

**Source quality and fact discipline.** Industry-published CAC benchmarks → H; aggregator and analyst reports → M; "we grew to X in Y" posts → L. Cite each benchmark at the point of use and pin a CAC figure once. Add competitor metrics and claims like "the channels are taken" or "that partnership is exclusive" to the claims-to-verify list; an unsourced claim like that is exactly the confident specific most likely to be wrong. **Survivorship bias is severe here:** failed GTM strategies don't get written up. Ask what the realistic distribution of outcomes is, not what worked for the winners.

**Local lens.** Search with local channel and platform names. Don't assume US channels, CAC ranges or ratios transfer. Watch for channels that dominate locally but barely exist in the US or Europe (WhatsApp Business in parts of LatAm, KakaoTalk, WeChat, regional ad platforms).

## Analytical work for this section

### Task 1: Generate candidate GTM strategies

Generate **2–4** candidate strategies suited to this idea's model, form factor and market. The question: *given who has to be acquired and what the unit economics can support, what are the realistic paths to traction?* Prompts, not a checklist:
- **Organic:** SEO, content, community, PR. Slow to compound, time-heavy, capital-light.
- **Paid:** search, social, programmatic, newsletter sponsorships. Fast, scales with capital, needs creative and optimization skill.
- **Direct:** outbound or founder-led sales, account-based marketing. Capital-light at low volume, capacity-heavy, fits higher-value B2B.
- **Partnerships:** distribution partners, resellers, integrations, embeds, associations.
- **Product-led:** referrals, virality, freemium conversion within the existing model, network effects.
- **Hybrids**, and patterns specific to the idea (events-led B2B, local presence).

Don't generate candidates that make no sense in the market (Google Ads where Google is not dominant wastes a slot). For each candidate:
- what it is, in one sentence;
- the channels involved;
- a realistic CAC range, from benchmarks;
- the time to impact;
- the capital it needs;
- the execution capacity it needs.

### Task 2: Evaluate each candidate

- **Can the unit economics afford this CAC?** Compare it with what the customer brings in, from 09. For pass-through models, use the **revenue the business keeps** per customer, not the gross. At this stage, say affordable, borderline or unaffordable; the payback test is in Task 6.
- **Does the time to impact fit the traction window** (Task 4)?
- **What capital does it need?** Report its capital intensity; it feeds the investment in Task 4.
- **Execution capacity:** can the founder execute it (per 10 and `user-profile.md`), or would it need hired or partner capacity? A capacity gap is **priced** (add the cost of the help to the investment), not used to rule the strategy out. The founder's own skills never decide whether the idea passes the gate.
- **Fit with the market:** does this channel actually work for these people here?

Output per candidate: viable, viable with adjustments, or not viable, with the reason.

### Task 3: Recommend a GTM strategy

Recommend the primary strategy and any viable alternates. If none is viable, say so, but **do not conclude that the gate fires here**; Task 6 attempts adjustments first.

### Task 4: Build the initial investment

**The window** is the time to MVP (from 10) plus a post-launch traction window set by the model:
- **~3 months** for consumer products with fast feedback loops;
- **~6 months** for transactional and self-serve models (the default);
- **~12 months** for B2B subscription or long sales cycles.

State the window used. Then combine four components, each conviction-labelled, and show the total:
- **Build:** the MVP capital from 10, plus partner or hired-help costs where 10 found gaps.
- **Compliance:** from 08, one-time setup plus ongoing compliance through the window. Everything that exists only because the activity is regulated goes here.
- **GTM:** the cost of executing the recommended strategy through the post-launch window (content, ad spend, sales costs, partner development), including any hired capacity from Task 2.
- **Operating:** infrastructure beyond the MVP, customer support, AI/LLM costs at expected post-launch volume, bookkeeping and general legal, banking, software subscriptions. These are the costs of running the business, regulated or not.

This is the capital needed to get from "decide to build" to "see whether it works". It is an **output**, the **launch investment**; 12 builds on it, and 12's cumulative capital is the runway the idea needs, which 13 reports. There is no founder-stated runway to compare against. Scaling capital, multi-year costs, scenarios and the threshold comparison belong to 12.

### Task 5: Apply the local lens

Cross-cutting market factors that affect GTM and investment as a whole:
- which channels actually work in this market;
- CAC and pricing dynamics: benchmarks from other markets rarely transfer;
- purchasing culture: B2B buying cycles, trust signals, the weight of word of mouth, sensitivity to advertising;
- the partnership ecosystem;
- local execution: paying for ads, agencies and marketing help.

If a finding reshapes the evaluation, revise Task 3's recommendation and say so.

### Task 6: Evaluate against the GTM gate

**Step 1: Test payback.** For each viable candidate, is CAC recoverable within the model's plausible payback window?
- **Subscription:** within 6–18 months of subscription revenue. Beyond that, the model is fragile to churn.
- **Transactional:** within a few transactions or quick repeat use. Recovery on the first transaction is ideal; within the first 3 months is acceptable.
- **B2B:** up to 24 months, if contract sizes and retention support it.
- **Other models:** the equivalent window for how the model actually earns.

**Step 2: If no candidate recovers CAC, try adjustments before concluding.** Work from least to most aggressive, documenting each attempt and its result. **Only adjustments that keep the idea the same idea** are allowed:
- **a different channel** within a strategy (different paid channels, a different sales intensity, a different partner type);
- **a pricing adjustment within 09's model** (a modest price change, tier restructuring, or packaging the same product differently, such as annual plans or multi-packs), not a different payer, product or payment pattern;
- **a beachhead focus** within the idea's own users and market (one city, region or vertical where acquisition is cheaper), as a starting point, not a new market.

**Not allowed:** a different audience or user, a different market, or a different model structure (unbundling, white-label, B2B2C, switching to freemium). Those are different ideas. If one would plausibly work and 04 has no such idea, flag that gap for 13 (`SKILL.md`, "Upstream/downstream dependencies").

**Step 3: Apply the gate.** It fires when, after the Step 2 attempt, **no candidate produces CAC recoverable within the model's plausible payback window** (canonical in `SKILL.md`, "The kill model").

**The gate does not fire** when:
- a candidate recovers CAC;
- CAC is uncertain but plausibly recoverable (12 will surface any problem);
- an allowed adjustment produced a viable candidate.

**If it fires,** the kill applies the challenge pass to itself and is confirmed per the conviction-gated rule (a kill resting on Low-conviction CAC benchmarks escalates). It ends **this idea**: 12 is skipped, 13 records it, and the run continues with the next selected idea, if any.

### Recommendation

One of two:
- **"Proceed to 12."**
- **"Kill — GTM gate."** 12 is skipped.

Findings the founder should weigh are **recorded, not surfaced mid-run**:
- CAC that recovers, but only just;
- an investment much larger than the build alone suggests;
- an adjustment that rescued the picture (say which);
- local factors that reshaped the GTM;
- capacity gaps that had to be priced in.

Name them in the Rationale; 13 reports them.

### Rationale

**For a proceed**, cover:
- the recommended strategy in one sentence (channel and acquisition pattern);
- the initial investment, total and components;
- the CAC payback against the model's window;
- the realistic time to meaningful traction.

**For a kill**, cover:
- why each candidate fails the payback test;
- the adjustments attempted and why each failed;
- the challenge pass applied to the kill;
- a note that it is confirmed per the conviction-gated rule.

### Challenge pass

The judging form from `SKILL.md` (06–13 omit the "non-obvious option" question), plus the section-specific questions below. For a kill, the pass asks what would make the kill wrong. Each finding uses the fixed short format: what changed and why, or "no change".

## Challenge pass: section-specific questions

- **CAC realism:** are the benchmarks from comparable products (same market, segment and acquisition pattern), or aspirational US numbers? A US enterprise SaaS CAC is not a benchmark for a LatAm SMB tool.
- **Survivorship check:** does the recommended strategy rest mostly on success stories? Investigate the failures before relying on it.
- **Capacity honesty:** were the founder's capacity gaps priced into the investment, or assumed away? "I'll learn paid acquisition while building the product" is rarely realistic.
- **Investment completeness:** are all four components there? Common omissions: AI costs at real volume, support beyond founder-handled tickets, bookkeeping and legal beyond compliance, hosting beyond the build budget, payment fees at post-launch volume.
- **Adjustment honesty and thoroughness** (when Step 2 ran): did an adjustment produce a genuinely viable candidate, or only optimistic assumptions? For a kill: were all allowed adjustments tried, alone and in combination?
